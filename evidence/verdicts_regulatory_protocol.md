# Policy and qualification basis

DECK: EU REACH broad PFAS restriction under review; final decision pending.
Minnesota: selected bans from 2025, broader prohibition 2032.
Maine: phased restrictions; timing and exemptions for cooling equipment differ.
This is the slide summary, not an equipment-specific legal determination.

Public references:
- ECHA PFAS overview: https://echa.europa.eu/hot-topics/perfluoroalkyl-chemicals-pfas
- Minnesota section 116.943: https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/116.943
- Maine Title 38 section 1614: https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/38/title38sec1614.html

DECK qualification roadmap:
1. Characterise the fluid against OCP thermal, electrical, chemical and safety criteria.
2. Soak seals, plastics, cables and metals at 80 ± 2 °C × 14 days with an untouched control.
3. Retest the soaked parts, then pilot a tank with real servers under load.

The compatibility soak is one exposure, not two successive ageing/soak stages.
Public method context: OCP Server Component Immersion Material Compatibility Testing v1.5 (June 2026), pp. 14–24. The roadmap is the talk's plan, not a claim that OCP mandates this entire pilot sequence.
https://www.opencompute.org/documents/ocp-component-compatibility-testing-guidelines-v1-5-final-june-5-2026-pdf

STD: OCP publishes application requirements; ASTM and IEC publish test methods.
Local fire codes, authorities and insurers also affect installation acceptance.
For an illustrative 10–15-year asset, policy may affect fluid supply, service and disposal.
Pure hydrocarbons contain no PFAS by molecular composition; additive packages require separate review.
